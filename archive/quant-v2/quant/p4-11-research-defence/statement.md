### The situation

You present the project to the head of crude research and a trader. The trader opens with a challenge:

> *"Our quant colleague's 'fair value' model for the Brent–WTI spread, fitted on 2012–2019, says WTI is 2 % rich today (log spread 0.06 vs fair 0.08). He wants to sell WTI against Brent. And yesterday, after a pipeline outage, WTI looked 3 % cheap against Brent. Your research says what, exactly?"*

You have the whole project: the hypothesis (4.1), OLS inference and model selection (4.2–4.3), ADF and the trend analysis (4.4–4.5), the spurious-regression check (4.6), Engle-Granger and the ECM (4.7–4.8), the regime comparison and the Chow test (4.9–4.10).

### What to write

A research conclusion of **14–20 sentences**, conclusion first, that covers:

1. the economic hypothesis and the data;
2. the transformations and the model you chose, and why (including what you rejected);
3. the statistical evidence, with numbers;
4. the economic interpretation — including your answer to both of the trader's situations;
5. the limits, the difference between statistical and economic significance, and what you would investigate next.

**Submit** your conclusion, then compare it with the reference answer and tick the key points you covered (self-review, not graded automatically).
