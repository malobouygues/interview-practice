### Reference answer

> **Hypothesis: WTI and Brent are tied by a long-run equilibrium — a fairly stable WTI discount — maintained by arbitrage, with deviations that close within days or weeks.**
>
> Both are light sweet crudes that refiners can largely substitute. When US crude can be exported, a WTI discount wider than the cost of moving barrels to the coast and across the Atlantic is an arbitrage: traders buy WTI, ship it and sell against Brent, which pulls the two prices back together. Brent is therefore the main explanatory variable. Dubai is a related benchmark driven by the same global balance, so it is probably redundant once Brent is in. Coffee has no mechanism linking it to crude and serves as a control: any "relationship" with coffee must be an artefact.
>
> If the hypothesis holds, daily returns should be strongly correlated, the log prices should drift together, and the log spread should fluctuate around a stable value instead of wandering. A high correlation of price levels would not be evidence: two trending prices correlate whether or not they are linked.
>
> The link can break when the arbitrage is blocked — an export ban, pipeline or storage bottlenecks at Cushing, a regional supply shock. Since exports were banned until December 2015, the relationship may be different before and after. I start with the exports-allowed regime, from mid-2016, and test that choice explicitly at the end.
>
> Method: correlation and simple OLS on returns; ADF on each series and on the spread; Engle-Granger and an error correction model on levels; then a comparison of sub-periods and a Chow test at the known date.

### What makes this a strong answer

- **A falsifiable hypothesis with a mechanism and a direction.**
- **Variables chosen for reasons**, including a deliberate control and an expected redundancy.
- **A predicted signature** — and an explicit statement of what would *not* count as evidence.
- **The sample choice is a hypothesis too**, to be tested at the end.

### Common weaknesses

- "They're both oil, so they're correlated" — no mechanism, no prediction.
- Starting with the full 2012–2019 sample without asking whether the regime changed.
- Listing methods without saying what each one tests.
