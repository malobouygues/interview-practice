### The desk asks

> “The diesel crack has widened materially. Is this consistent with tighter physical supply, or are we looking at a data problem or a misleading statistical relationship? Give me your view in a few paragraphs — I'll read it before the morning meeting.”

### What you know (from 2.1–2.4)

- The vendor data had real problems: a frozen gasoil feed, a RBOB price sent in cents under a dollar label, a 0.00 gasoil print, duplicates, mixed units, and import statistics where non-reporting countries appear as zeros.
- 25 extreme daily moves were investigated: 2 errors repaired, 22 kept as events or market-wide moves, 1 kept as doubtful with a sensitivity check.
- The monthly gasoil crack correlates at −0.96 with stocks against their 5-year average; OLS gives about −1.1 $/bbl per point of deficit; White's test rejects constant variance; HC3 standard errors are 1.5–1.6 times larger for two of the coefficients; residuals show a pattern in the tightest months.
