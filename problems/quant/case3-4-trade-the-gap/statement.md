### The situation

It is the morning after a US-specific supply scare. The log Brent–WTI spread stands at **0.090**, against an average of **≈ 0.059** over 2016–2019. A trader comes to your desk:

> *"Your cointegration stuff says WTI and Brent are tied together, right? WTI looks 3 % too cheap. Should I buy WTI and sell Brent? How long until it closes, and what could go wrong?"*

You have the Engle-Granger result (step 3.2: β ≈ 0.98, statistic −7.4) and the ECM (step 3.3: α ≈ −0.115, γ ≈ 0.89, half-life ≈ 5.7 business days).

### What to write

An answer of **8–14 sentences** that a trader can act on. It should:

- translate the 3 % into the language of the model (error-correction term) and say what the ECM implies for the expected convergence and its timing;
- connect the statistical result to the economic mechanism, and say what you would want to know about *why* the gap opened;
- state clearly what the model cannot guarantee — statistical, model and regime risks;
- say what would make this a sensible trade or not (size of the expected move, costs, risk control).
